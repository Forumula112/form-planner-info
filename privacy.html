Form Planner privacy policy

Effective date: November 13, 2026.

What this policy covers

This policy describes the Google Calendar connection in the Windows desktop version of Form Planner. The app is currently intended for the owner's personal use. There is no Form Planner server collecting Calendar data.

Data the app accesses

With your permission, Form Planner requests this Google scope:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events.readonly

It reads events from your primary calendar, starting today in Edmonton through the next 45 days, including recurring instances. Google responses can include event titles, descriptions, dates, times, event IDs, source links and organizer, creator or attendee metadata. The app may use Google's self metadata to identify the connected account. It does not request Gmail, contacts, Calendar write or additional profile permissions.

Google authorization and token services receive the requests needed to grant, verify, refresh and revoke access. Calendar requests are sent directly from the desktop app to Google over HTTPS. Google's handling of these requests is governed by Google's privacy policy.

Why the app uses this data

The app uses Calendar data to display your events, identify the connected account, refresh the calendar view, prevent duplicate display records and preserve the last successful view when offline. It does not change Google Calendar events.

The app does not sell Calendar data, use it for advertising, send it to AI services, or use it to train machine-learning models. It has no feature that sends Calendar data to the app developer or other users. Opening a source link sends your browser to Google Calendar.

Form Planner's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Storage and protection

The OAuth client configuration and retained authorization grant are encrypted using Electron's Windows-protected credential storage. The renderer does not receive tokens or client secrets. If that protection is unavailable, the app does not save authorization in plaintext.

The local Calendar cache contains event titles, descriptions, dates, source links, event identifiers, a connected-account identifier and sync metadata. The app does not encrypt this cache. It is stored in your Windows user profile, separately from your planner data. People or software with access to your Windows account or files may be able to read it. Protect your computer and any copies of its files.

The app does not include its Google credentials or direct Calendar cache in ordinary planner backup exports. Separately imported records and any copies you make yourself are outside that cache and may contain Calendar information.

Retention and removal

A successful sync replaces the cached event window. Event IDs can be retained to prevent previously imported records from appearing twice. Failed requests retain the last successful cache.

Choose Disconnect & revoke in the app's Google Calendar settings to stop local access and clear its active Calendar cache. The app removes its stored grant after Google confirms revocation. If Google is unreachable, it retains encrypted revocation material solely to retry while the app runs. You can also revoke access through your Google Account. The imported OAuth client configuration remains available for later setup.

Disconnecting does not delete your Google events, planner records, previously imported snapshots or independent backups. Removing Google access outside the app stops future authorized reads but does not itself remove existing local files.

Changes and contact

This policy must be updated before the app changes how it accesses, uses, stores or shares Google data. Any new permission requires the applicable Google consent process.
